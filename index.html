Research interests

Membrane excitability

Theory & experiment
Dynamic clamp · long-term recordings · models

The study of excitability, from Galvani and Helmholtz, through Adrian, Hodgkin and Huxley, Neher and Sakmann, all the way to MacKinnon’s elucidation of a channel protein structure, should no doubt make us physiologists very proud of our discipline. But there is still a long way to go.

Present concepts and technologies make it possible to implement the biophysical understanding of excitability in the more general context of mechanisms underlying the emergence and maintenance of functional cellular organization. Accordingly, the body of our theoretical and experimental studies reflects the idea of membrane excitability as a toy model for self-organization of cellular function.

These studies address parameterisation of high-dimensional models of excitability, adaptation over extended ranges of time scales, critical self-organization, multiplicity of system states and its impacts on scaling of rates, modeling state-dependent processes, response entrainment, resilience of function to parametric variations, and the effects of spatial extension (e.g., axonal conduction) on the dynamics of excitability.

  • high-dimensional models
  • adaptation across time scales
  • self-organized criticality
  • state multiplicity
  • response entrainment
  • parametric resilience
  • axonal conduction

        10-string · recordings

        Ten strings, one take

        Yepes tuning
        MacBook Pro microphone

        These are one-take home recordings on a Michael Gee ten-string classical guitar in Yepes tuning, presented as played, without edits. They are modest sketches, made with the internal microphone of a MacBook Pro, and are intended to convey the resonance and phrasing of this rare instrument. The built-in microphone captures its lowest register only partially.
